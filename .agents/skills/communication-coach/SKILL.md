# Communication coach

Most work messages are read in a few seconds on a small screen. The reader should know what you need and by when before they finish the first line.

## When to use

- The user pastes a draft message and asks to improve it.
- The user describes what they want to say and asks for a draft.
- A status update, decision note, piece of feedback or request feels too long or unclear.

## Steps

1. Identify the reader and the goal. Who reads it, what they already know, and what you want them to do or understand afterward. If the reader is not clear, ask one short question or make a stated assumption.

2. Name the message type and its lead.
   - Ask: the request, who it is for, and the deadline.
   - Decision: what was decided, then the main reason, then what changes.
   - Status: on track or not, then what moved, then what is blocked and what you need.
   - Feedback: the specific behavior, its effect, and what to do differently.

3. Put the lead in the first sentence. Move background below it, or cut it.

4. Cut filler. Remove openers like "just wanted to", "I think maybe", "hope this helps", and repeated context the reader already has. Keep the facts, names, numbers and dates.

5. Make it scannable. Short paragraphs. A list only when there are three or more parallel items. Bold only the one thing the reader must not miss, if anything.

6. Match the reader. Senior readers get the outcome and the decision needed. Close collaborators get the detail they will act on. Keep the user's own voice and level of formality.

7. Check tone. Direct is good. Blunt is not. Feedback names the work, never the person. An ask says why it matters to the reader when that is not obvious.

## Output format

Give the rewrite first, in a code block so it can be copied as is. Then two or three short notes.

```text
Can you approve the staging budget increase by Thursday? Without it the load test slips a week.

We need 2 more test servers for the run on the 18th. Cost is about 300 dollars for the month.
```

What changed:
- Moved the ask and the deadline to the first line.
- Cut the two background paragraphs to one line on cost and timing.
- Removed the apology at the end.

## Rules

- Keep the user's meaning. Do not add claims, numbers or promises they did not make.
- Keep it shorter than the original unless the original was missing something the reader needs. Say what you added and why.
- Offer one rewrite by default. Give alternatives only when the right tone depends on something you do not know.
- Coaching only. Do not send, post or schedule the message.

## Done when

- The first sentence carries the ask or the point.
- Filler is gone and the facts are intact.
- The message fits the reader and keeps the user's voice.
- The user has one rewrite and two or three notes on what changed.
